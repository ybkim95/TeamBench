\paragraph{TeamBench-Mini Representativeness.}
TeamBench-Mini is a curated subset of 28 tasks drawn from the full benchmark.
Of these, 22 tasks appear in our regression dataset (147 tasks); the remaining 6
(D1, P1, SPEC1, INC1, IR1, NEG1) are absent from the regression CSV but are
included in the ablation runs.
The 28 Mini tasks span 13 of 23 benchmark categories (57\%), covering
\textit{code\_review}, \textit{data}, \textit{incident}, \textit{ir},
\textit{multi\_layer}, \textit{negotiation}, \textit{ops}, \textit{other},
\textit{pipeline}, \textit{policy}, \textit{security}, \textit{spec\_driven},
and \textit{testing}.
Categories absent from Mini include \textit{adversarial}, \textit{cross\_system},
\textit{crypto}, \textit{distributed}, \textit{github\_real}, \textit{integration},
\textit{legalhold}, \textit{multilang}, \textit{scaling}, and \textit{swe}.

With respect to oracle performance, Mini tasks are intentionally harder than the
full benchmark: the Mini mean oracle score is 0.375 ($\sigma=0.293$) versus
0.575 ($\sigma=0.326$) for the full set, a gap of $-0.20$ points.
A two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test confirms this distributional difference
is statistically significant ($D=0.374$, $p=0.007$), indicating that Mini
overrepresents tasks where a single capable agent already struggles.
Difficulty-wise, Mini skews medium (54.5\% vs.\ 27.2\% in the full benchmark)
and underrepresents hard tasks (40.9\% vs.\ 61.9\%) and expert tasks (0\% vs.\ 6.8\%).

Despite this oracle-score shift, the \emph{team uplift} distributions are
statistically indistinguishable between Mini and the full benchmark
(KS test: $D=0.170$, $p=0.572$).
Mean uplift is $+8.0\%$ for Mini versus $+1.7\%$ for the full set,
and the fraction of \textit{team-helps} tasks (uplift $>0.05$) is
36.4\% (Mini) versus 36.7\% (Full), confirming that Mini preserves
the signal of interest---the relative benefit of team coordination---even
though it was not sampled to match the full oracle score distribution.
In summary, TeamBench-Mini is a \emph{difficulty-stratified} subset that
faithfully captures teamwork dynamics while concentrating evaluation effort
on tasks where inter-agent coordination provides measurable value.
