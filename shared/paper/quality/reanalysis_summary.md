# TeamBench reanalysis: retiring TNI and rebuilding the statistics

Generated by `scripts/reanalysis.py` on the 155-task reference ablation, the LB90 leaderboard pool, and the role-mixing run pool. Every figure below is recomputed from primitive per-run condition scores; no stored derived field is trusted.

## 0. One mechanism, three failures

A single run's per-task necessity gap has reliability **0.06** on the multi-seed subset: 94 percent of the apparent task-to-task variation in how much the information partition is worth is run-to-run noise, not task structure. That one fact generates all three problems below, and it is a property of the measurement design rather than of any analysis choice made afterwards.

1. **The ratio.** TNI divides a difference by that noisy, near-zero quantity. Its denominator has mean +0.0073 (t = 0.47) and is exactly zero on 87 of 155 tasks, so no bounded confidence set for the ratio exists.
2. **The stratification.** Conditioning on that same noisy run and then reporting a difference that contains it produces a monotone capability gradient out of nothing: the exact no-effect null of the published design gives a Q1-minus-Q5 spread of +38.8 pp against the published +24.7 pp.
3. **The admission count.** The between-task variance in partition value is not distinguishable from zero once run-to-run noise is subtracted, so a rule that counts tasks whose single-run estimate clears a threshold is counting noise excursions. On this corpus that count is 0.148 of tasks at a 10-point threshold and the corpus supports 0.000.

On the one place where a capability-conditional signal might still live -- stratifying on a difficulty estimate that shares no run with the contrast -- the evidence is marginal and does not replicate across the three instruments. What would settle it: three or more seeds per task per condition on the full pool. At one run per cell the difficulty instrument and the contrast are both noise-dominated, and no amount of re-analysis can separate a small real gradient from sampling variation.

Against that, the effect the aggregate was hiding is real and survives: the team wins 68 of 99 contested tasks (sign test p = 2.6e-04) while its mean advantage is -0.26 pp, because it loses by 2.26 times as much as it wins by.

## 1. Why TNI is not estimable on this pool

### 1.1 The denominator is statistically zero

- Necessity gap (Solo minus Restricted) over 155 tasks: mean **+0.0073**, SD 0.1914, t = **0.474**, p = 0.636.
- Exactly zero on **87 of 155 tasks** (56 percent); negative on 27, positive on 41.
- On **118 of 155 tasks** (76 percent) the paper's epsilon floor is the binding value, so the denominator is a constant the authors chose, not a measurement.

### 1.2 The ratio adds nothing the difference does not already carry

- Identity, exact to 2.8e-17 on every task: `(Team - Restricted) = (Solo - Restricted) + (Team - Solo)`.
- Therefore **TNI = 1 + (S_team - S_solo)/(S_solo - S_restricted)**.
- On tasks with a positive gap, the claim "TNI > 1" is algebraically identical to "Team beats Solo". The ratio contributes no information beyond the sign of the difference, and it contributes unbounded variance.

### 1.3 The shipped implementations disagree with each other and with the paper

Four code paths compute something called TNI, and none of them computes Eq. (1) as written. The last row is not shipped anywhere; it is the raw ratio, included so the effect of each guard is visible.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `paper_eq1_eps0.05` | 155/155 | -0.419 | -20.00 | 15.53 |
| `compute_tni_py` | 61/155 | 0.631 | -2.00 | 2.00 |
| `ablation_py_eps0.01` | 155/155 | -3.691 | -100.00 | 77.67 |
| `validate_tni_py` | 155/155 | -1.058 | -100.00 | 1.00 |

On the identical 61-task subset the paper's Eq. (1) returns **-1.080** and `harness/compute_tni.py` returns **+0.631**, with 14 sign disagreements. `harness/validate_tni.py` computes a different estimand entirely and publishes it under the same name.

`shared/paper/ablation_summary.json` is internally inconsistent: its per-task block admits 61 tasks (mean +0.631) while its own aggregate block admits 37 (mean +0.744).

### 1.4 The clamp is load-bearing and undocumented

- 9 values sit on +2 and 3 on -2, which is **20 percent of the 61 reported values**.
- Removing the clamp moves the mean from +0.631 to +0.923, with an unclamped range of [-9.2, 10.0].
- The clamp appears nowhere in the paper.

An earlier internal note recorded the paper formula at -0.745 against the code's +0.651 on the same tasks, and 11 of 61 stored values on the clamp. Recomputing from shared/paper/ablation_summary.json gives -1.080 against +0.631 on the 61-task subset the code admits, and 12 of 61 values on a clamp. The direction and the magnitude of the disagreement are the same; the exact figures differ because the earlier note used a different admission filter. We report the recomputed values.

### 1.5 A valid interval for the ratio is unbounded

- Fieller construction on the ratio of means: point estimate +0.639, denominator t = 0.474 (critical value 1.975).
- Quadratic leading coefficient a = -0.00086934 <= 0, so the 95 percent confidence set is **(-inf, +inf)** -- the ratio is not estimable at this sample size.
- The bootstrap distribution of the *mean of per-task ratios* on the 61 defined tasks spans [-0.4, 2.2] with a 95 percent width of 1.46.

Observed denominator mean on this pool: +0.0073, far to the left of the leftmost grid point. Two things happen as the denominator approaches zero, and only one of them is a coverage failure. First, the estimator's dispersion explodes: the standard deviation of the point estimate grows by three orders of magnitude across this grid, so the number reported as 'average TNI' is dominated by which side of zero the denominator's sampling error lands on. Second, the percentile bootstrap's coverage degrades below nominal only in the extreme left of the grid; elsewhere it holds nominal coverage by producing intervals so wide they exclude nothing. Neither regime yields a usable estimate. Fieller is the honest report: it returns an unbounded set precisely when the data cannot pin the ratio down, and the fraction of unbounded sets rises from 0 to over 90 percent as the denominator shrinks.

On the 39-task multi-seed subset, a single run's necessity gap has reliability **0.06** (within-task variance 0.0436 against between-task variance 0.0029).

## 2. The replacement: the Coordination Necessity Contrast

**Coordination Necessity Contrast (CNC)**. 2-D per task: (g_i, u_i). Never a ratio.

- `g_i` = S_solo,i - S_restricted,i  (partition value: how much score the withheld information is worth to a single agent under the same turn budget)
- `u_i` = S_team,i - S_solo,i  (team advantage over the compute-matched single agent that has everything)

g and u answer different questions and are not commensurable. g is a property of the task's information design: if g = 0 the task never had a coordination requirement to detect, regardless of what the team does. u is a property of the multi-agent system. Dividing one by the other destroys both, because the denominator is a design property that is legitimately zero on many tasks.

- Estimator: sample mean of within-task differences.
- Interval: percentile bootstrap resampling TASKS (the cluster), conditions carried together so the contrast stays paired.
- Null: exact within-task sign-flip permutation; the estimator is centred at 0 under the null and its permutation p-value is valid without any distributional assumption.

| quantity | mean (pp) | 95% CI (pp) | permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|
| partition value g | +0.73 | [-2.38, +3.62] | 0.653 |
| team advantage u | -0.26 | [-5.11, +4.52] | 0.916 |
| relay advantage (old TNI numerator) | +0.47 | [-4.64, +5.58] | 0.855 |
| planner value | +2.29 | [-3.07, +8.11] | 0.416 |
| verifier value | -5.43 | [-10.04, -0.79] | 0.026 |

**A zero mean is not no effect.** Of 155 tasks the contrast is tied on 56. Among the 99 contested tasks the team wins **68** and loses 31, a win rate of 0.687 [0.590, 0.770], sign test p = 2.55e-04. But it gains +19.9 pp when it wins and loses -44.9 pp when it loses, a magnitude ratio of 2.26. The two cancel to a mean of -0.26 pp.

The team wins more contested tasks than it loses, and the sign test on that count is significant, but the mean contrast is zero because the losses are larger than the wins. This is a variance effect, not an absence of an effect: adding roles changes the distribution of outcomes without moving its centre. Reporting only the mean hides it, and so does reporting only the win count.


Two-dimensional census at tau = 0.05: **4** tasks are coordination-demanding (positive partition value *and* positive team advantage); 11 have a real partition the team fails to exploit; 24 have an inverted partition where Restricted beats Solo.

### 2.1 Benchmark admission as a probability, not a count

Measurement variance of the contrast, from the 39-task replicate subset: sigma2_e = 0.0436. Task-to-task variance of the single-run estimates on the full pool: 0.0366. Implied between-task variance: -0.0070 on the full pool and +0.0029 on the replicate subset.

> The estimated between-task variance in theta_g is not positive: the run-to-run variance measured on the replicate subset (0.0436) is at least as large as the task-to-task variance of the single-run estimates on the full pool (0.0366). Both decompositions agree on the sign. Under the model this means the corpus provides no evidence that any task has more of this quantity than any other, so every task's posterior collapses onto the grand mean, and the grand mean itself is 0.0073 with a 95% interval of [-0.0227, 0.0372] that contains zero. The correct conclusion is not that admission is easy or impossible, but that at one run per task the corpus cannot rank its own tasks by this quantity at all.

Runs per cell needed to make a single task's estimate reliable, under the optimistic between-task variance 0.0029: reliability 0.5 needs 16 runs, reliability 0.7 needs 36 runs, reliability 0.8 needs 61 runs (the reference ablation has 1).


Sensitivity to the measurement-variance estimate:

| assumed sigma2_e | implied reliability of one run | pi(0.10) | pi(0.20) |
|---|---|---|---|
| sigma2_e x 0.25 (0.0109) | 0.702 | 0.232 | 0.086 |

| sigma2_e x 1.0 (0.0436) | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

The tau = 0 row is not informative: asking whether a task's true necessity exceeds zero is asking for the sign of a quantity the corpus cannot measure, and at the variance boundary the estimator correctly returns the sign of the grand mean for every task. The substantive reading is in the sensitivity block, and it is not that the hierarchical number is small. It is that the admitted fraction at a 10-point threshold moves across the whole available range as the assumed measurement variance moves over a four-fold band, and that band is not resolvable from 39 tasks with three seeds. The admitted fraction of this corpus is therefore not identified, and no threshold on a single run per condition can identify it. The naive count is a point on that curve chosen by assuming the measurement variance is zero, which is the one value the replicate data rule out. The fix is replication, not a better threshold. The runs-per-cell block gives the budget: under the optimistic between-task variance estimate, ranking tasks by partition value needs the stated number of runs per cell before a single task's estimate is even half reliable, against the one run per cell the reference ablation currently has.


Under an exact no-effect null:
- tau=0.0: naive count rule still admits 0.220 of tasks; the hierarchical rule admits 0.507.
- tau=0.05: naive count rule still admits 0.198 of tasks; the hierarchical rule admits 0.001.
- tau=0.1: naive count rule still admits 0.130 of tasks; the hierarchical rule admits 0.000.

## 3. The quintile result, redone

Published design reproduced: profile [15.74, 8.82, -6.83, -10.08, -8.97] pp, Q1 minus Q5 = **+24.71 pp**.
(Stored published profile: [15.74, 8.82, -6.83, -10.08, -8.97]; max reproduction difference 0.00 pp.)

The stratifier and the minuend of the contrast are the same single run. Selecting the lowest bin selects tasks whose Solo run was unlucky; the Team run does not share that luck, so the difference is positive by construction, and symmetrically negative in the top bin. This is regression to the mean, and its magnitude is set by the run-to-run noise share of the Solo score.

Under the exact null (swap Team and Solo labels within each task, 5000 permutations) the *same design* produces a mean profile of **[26.43, 3.1, -5.39, -11.77, -12.33] pp** and a Q1-minus-Q5 spread of **+38.76 pp** (5th to 95th percentile +30.2 to +47.5). Observed spread +24.71 pp; p(null at least this steep) = 0.9970, p(null this flat or flatter) = 0.0032.

The observed spread is in fact SMALLER than the null spread (p = 0.0032 in the lower tail). The design's artifact overshoots the data: after accounting for regression to the mean, the capability-conditional effect points weakly the other way.

**RTM-corrected effect: -14.04 pp** (uncorrected +24.71 pp; the design's own artifact accounts for 1.57 times the published spread).

| I1_restricted_run | 155 | [11.17, 6.66, -16.94, 1.85, -4.06] | +15.23 | -0.70 | -0.01 | 0.031 | 0.092 | -4.91 | 0.077 |
| I2_mean_of_lesioned_team_arms | 155 | [-1.54, 0.46, -0.44, 4.55, -4.34] | +2.81 | -0.10 | +0.17 | 0.692 | 0.901 | -0.17 | 0.969 |
| I3_heldout_models_solo | 54 | [-1.97, 2.94, -8.24, 0.79, -14.3] | +12.33 | -0.50 | +0.18 | 0.451 | 0.901 | -3.07 | 0.969 |

The Q1-minus-Q5 contrast is a two-point comparison and can be large on a profile with no monotone trend. The continuous trend test uses every task and does not depend on where the bin edges fall, so it is the primary test; the quintile contrast is reported for comparability with the published figure. Both are corrected across the three instruments by Holm.

**No capability-conditional team effect survives stratification on an independent difficulty estimate once the three instruments are corrected for multiplicity. The published effect is a property of the estimator, not of the systems it was applied to. The closest call is I1_restricted_run, whose uncorrected p is 0.026 and whose Holm-corrected p across the three instruments is above 0.05. Two further reasons not to promote it: it is the noisiest instrument of the three (a single run), and the least noisy instrument (two runs averaged) gives a slope of -0.17 pp against its -4.91 pp. Independent stratifier noise attenuates a true gradient without biasing it, so a real gradient must show up at least as strongly in the less noisy instrument; the observed ordering is the opposite. Whatever residual signal is there is also an order of magnitude smaller than the published +24.7 pp contrast.**

## 4. The uplift, relabelled

`harness/compute_tni.py, TaskMetrics.team_uplift` computes `return self.team_partial - self.restricted_partial`. The field is named team_uplift and is consumed downstream as the team-versus-Solo effect, but it is the team-versus-Restricted effect. Restricted is a handicapped single agent that never sees the specification, so this comparison flatters the team by the full value of the information partition.

| comparison | mean (pp) | 95% CI (pp) | permutation p | team wins / loses / ties |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full Team minus Solo | -0.26 | [-5.28, +4.63] | 0.915 | 68 / 31 / 56 |
| Full Team minus Restricted | +0.47 | [-4.74, +5.41] | 0.861 | 75 / 28 / 52 |

paper/v7/neurips_2026.tex, Sec. 5: "Mean team-vs-Solo uplift on the reference ablation is +0.5 points (p=0.20, paired bootstrap, n=10,000), and the team wins on 68 of 155 tasks."

The two halves of that sentence come from different comparisons. The win count of 68 is Full minus Solo. The +0.5 points is Full minus Restricted. Full minus Solo is negative, and Full minus Restricted wins on 75 tasks, not 68. The sentence is internally consistent only if the two quantities are the same comparison, and they are not.


On LB90, paired within task, Restricted beats Solo for **13 of 16 models**; pooled Restricted minus Solo = +8.27 pp.

The stored LB90 aggregate reports each condition's rate over whatever tasks completed for that condition, so per-condition denominators differ within a model. Any Solo-vs-Team comparison read off those rates is unpaired and confounded with which tasks happened to finish. The paired numbers here restrict to the task intersection within each model.

## 5. Statistical machinery audit

**Pairing.** harness/statistics.py::bootstrap_ci_difference resamples the two arms independently. That is an unpaired bootstrap. The paper describes its bootstrap as paired. Arms correlate at r = 0.622 across tasks. Paired SE 2.51 pp against unpaired 4.06 pp, a ratio of 1.61.

**Clustering.** The role-mixing pool has 2145 runs nested in 25 tasks (85.8 runs per task). ICC(1,1) on pass is 0.3845, design effect **33.61**, effective n 63.8. A Wilson interval on the raw run count is roughly 5.75 times too narrow.

**Multiplicity.** Multiplicity is corrected inside one family of three tests (scripts/statistical_robustness.py applies Holm-Bonferroni there) while the rest of the paper's confirmatory tests -- per-condition contrasts, per-category uplift, per-quintile contrasts, per-model leaderboard comparisons -- are reported uncorrected. Correcting one family and not the others is not a correction.

| test | raw p | Holm | BH | survives Holm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| partition_value_g_nonzero | 0.6393 | 1.0000 | 0.9145 | False |
| team_advantage_u_nonzero | 0.9145 | 1.0000 | 0.9145 | False |
| relay_advantage_nonzero | 0.8608 | 1.0000 | 0.9145 | False |
| planner_value_nonzero | 0.4166 | 1.0000 | 0.9145 | False |
| verifier_value_nonzero | 0.0287 | 0.1437 | 0.1437 | False |

Declare one confirmatory family in the protocol (the five condition contrasts), correct it, and label everything else explicitly exploratory. Per-category and per-quintile numbers should carry no stars at all.

## 6. Data provenance of the canonical table, and robustness to repairing it

**Silent binarisation.** `harness/paper_tables.py, runs_to_task_metrics` evaluates

```python
partial = run.get("partial_score", 1.0 if run.get("pass") else 0.0)
```

Runs produced by the multi-seed phase-3 campaign store the partial score at run['secondary']['partial_score'] and carry no top-level 'partial_score' key. The default branch fires and the run is scored 1.0/0.0. Those binarised values are then averaged with seed 0's genuine partial score, so the canonical per-task number is a mean of one continuous and up to two dichotomised measurements.

This fires on **380 runs**, touching **190 of 775 cells** of the canonical per-task table.

Worked example, `CR5_test_coverage` / `restricted`: seed 0 scores 0.38; seeds 1 and 2 score [0.54, 0.31] but are read as [0.0, 0.0]. The table stores **0.12667** where reading the partial scores gives **0.41000**.

**Model provenance.** The glob has no model filter. The table captioned as a single ablation draws on gemini-2.5-flash files (the original 20-task pool), gemini-3-flash-preview files (the expansion), and, in the directory's current state, cross-model runs from other models entirely. Runs by declared model in the merge:

- `gemini-3-flash-preview`: 1190
- `gemini-2.5-flash`: 90
- `vllm:google/codegemma-7b-it@http://localhost:8005/v1`: 35

**Reproducibility.** Re-running the documented merge over the 106 files it globs today reproduces 740 of 775 cells; 35 now differ. Cells that do not reproduce are ones where a file added after the table was generated now wins the first-wins dedup. That is itself the finding: the canonical table is not reproducible from the current contents of the directory it globs.

## 7. Where the multi-agent gain lives: the pass line, not the graded score

The apparent multi-agent gain is scale-dependent. On the pass criterion the team is ahead; on the graded score it is not. That is a decomposition, not a contradiction, and it explains why different analyses in the same repository disagreed about the sign of the same effect. Any multi-agent benchmark that reports one scale and not the other can report a gain or a loss at will.

Provenance-controlled pool, 107 tasks, both scales on the same runs:

| contrast | scale | mean (pp) | 95% CI (pp) | perm p | wins / losses / ties |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full_minus_solo | partial score in [0,1] | +0.24 | [-4.88, +5.40] | 0.925 | 49 / 18 / 40 |
| full_minus_solo | grader pass/fail | +17.76 | [+9.35, +27.10] | 0.000 | 22 / 3 / 82 |
| full_minus_restricted | partial score in [0,1] | +3.61 | [-2.27, +9.15] | 0.232 | 52 / 14 / 41 |
| full_minus_restricted | grader pass/fail | +19.63 | [+11.21, +28.04] | 0.000 | 23 / 2 / 82 |
| solo_minus_restricted | partial score in [0,1] | +3.38 | [-0.84, +7.65] | 0.122 | 26 / 16 / 65 |
| solo_minus_restricted | grader pass/fail | +1.87 | [-2.80, +7.48] | 0.740 | 5 / 3 / 99 |

Pass transitions on the same 107 tasks: the team passes where Solo fails on **22** tasks and Solo passes where the team fails on 3 (exact paired test p = 0.0002); 13 tasks are passed by both and 69 by neither. On the tasks neither arm passes, the team's graded score is -2.84 pp against Solo.

The team converts failures into passes more often than the reverse, and simultaneously loses graded credit on the tasks where neither arm reaches the pass line. A benchmark reported only as pass rate sees the first effect; one reported only as graded score sees the second. Reporting both is the finding, not a caveat.

**These runs are not compute-matched.** Mean wall clock by condition: oracle 92s, restricted 80s, full 410s, team_no_plan 152s, team_no_verify 112s; the Full Team spends 4.43 times the single agent's wall clock, and the per-task pass gain correlates 0.066 with the per-task elapsed-time gap.

harness/ablation.py::run_ablation_condition documents the pre-equalisation configuration in its own docstring: Solo ran at 20 turns against a Full Team at up to 140. The runs in this pool predate the shared TurnBudget, so they carry that gap.

The pass-scale advantage above is NOT compute-matched. It is measured on runs where the team was allowed several times the single agent's turn allowance, so the correct reading is that the multi-agent gain that this benchmark reports lives on the pass criterion AND is measured under a compute asymmetry. Those are the two confounds to separate, and separating them requires re-running the pool under the shared budget. Nothing here decides how much of the pass-scale gain survives equalisation.

On the 39-task multi-seed subset (seeds 1 and 2 (the seeds that carry a partial score under the correct key)), the same contrast is +17.95 pp on the pass scale [+6.41, +30.77], p = 0.011, and +13.32 pp on the graded scale [+9.87, +16.89], p = 0.000.

shared/paper/statistical_robustness.json reports Full minus Solo as +20.5 points on this subset with p = 0.0024. That analysis is on the binary pass scale and pools seeds 0-2 as per-task means. The graded-score contrast on the same runs is the other column.

The graded-score contrast is null on the full provenance-controlled pool and positive on the 39-task multi-seed subset. Those are different task pools, not different scales: the multi-seed subset was selected for re-running and is a quarter of the size. Read the scale decomposition off the full pool and treat the subset as a separate, smaller estimate rather than a replication of it.

## 8. What changes in the paper

- Delete Eq. (1) and every TNI number. Replace with the two-dimensional Coordination Necessity Contrast (g, u), each reported as a paired task-clustered mean with a bootstrap CI.
- Replace 'mean team uplift +0.5 points' with the two separate quantities: Full minus Solo = -0.26 pp [-5.28, +4.63] and Full minus Restricted = +0.47 pp [-4.74, +5.41].
- Replace the quintile figure. The exact no-effect null of the same design produces a STEEPER profile than the published one (null Q1-minus-Q5 +38.76 pp against a published +24.71 pp), so the RTM-corrected contrast is -14.04 pp and the published gradient is fully accounted for by the estimator.
- Add the win/loss asymmetry, which is a real result the mean was hiding: the team wins 68 of 99 contested tasks (sign test p = 2.6e-04) while the mean contrast is -0.26 pp, because losses run 2.26 times larger than wins. Report (win rate, mean effect) as a pair.
- Add the scale decomposition as a headline: on the provenance-controlled pool the same runs give a large positive multi-agent effect on the pass criterion (+17.8 pp) and none on the graded score (+0.2 pp). That reconciles the two contradictory numbers already in the repository and is the mechanism behind the win/loss asymmetry. State in the same breath that those runs predate the shared turn budget, so the pass-scale gain is confounded with compute and the equalised re-run is what decides how much of it is coordination.
- Report the data-provenance repair. The canonical table averages binarised multi-seed scores with continuous seed-0 scores on 190 of 775 cells and draws on more than one model; the provenance-controlled pool is the one to publish.
- Report benchmark admission as a hierarchical probability with a stated threshold, not as a count of tasks whose single-run estimate clears a line.
- State the bootstrap that was actually run. The library function resamples arms independently; the paper calls it paired.
- Quote cluster-adjusted intervals for any rate pooled over runs nested in tasks, and give the design effect alongside.
- Declare a single confirmatory family, correct it, and mark per-category, per-quintile and per-model numbers as exploratory.

## 9. What this analysis cannot establish

- The reference ablation has one run per (task, condition) at seed 0, so per-task estimates carry run-to-run noise that only the 39-task multi-seed subset can quantify. Every shrinkage and RTM figure above imports its measurement variance from that subset and assumes it transfers to the full pool.
- The held-out-model difficulty instrument covers only the tasks the reference ablation and LB90 share, so its quintiles are coarser than the full-pool ones.
- The permutation nulls test exchangeability of condition labels within a task. They do not test whether the graders measure what the tasks intend, which is a separate validity question handled by the admission gate.
